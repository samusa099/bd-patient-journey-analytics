# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We want **BD Patient Journey Analytics** to be a respectful, inclusive, and safe learning and collaboration space for contributors from healthcare operations, HR, analytics, software, finance, and related disciplines.

## Expected behaviour

Contributors should:

- communicate respectfully and professionally;
- welcome questions, constructive disagreement, and different levels of experience;
- focus criticism on ideas, code, data, and process rather than people;
- protect privacy and never share real patient-identifying, biometric, credential, financial, or other sensitive personal data;
- clearly distinguish synthetic demonstrations from real clinical or hospital implementations;
- respect clinical boundaries: operational analytics and AI must not be presented as a substitute for qualified clinical judgement;
- document assumptions and limitations when proposing analytical, financial, workforce, or routing changes; and
- follow repository security, licensing, and contribution requirements.

## Unacceptable behaviour

The following are not acceptable:

- harassment, discrimination, intimidation, threats, or personal attacks;
- publishing another person's private information without permission;
- knowingly committing real patient records, credentials, secrets, biometric templates, or sensitive identifiers;
- presenting synthetic or simulated outputs as verified real-world medical evidence;
- deliberately disrupting discussions, reviews, issues, or project workflows; or
- repeated bad-faith behaviour after maintainers have asked for correction.

## Scope

This code applies to repository issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, project documentation, community spaces, and other interactions representing this project.

## Reporting and enforcement

If you observe behaviour that violates this code, report it privately to the repository maintainer through an appropriate private GitHub contact channel. Do not post sensitive details publicly.

Maintainers may edit or remove contributions, close or lock discussions, reject pull requests, or restrict participation when necessary to protect the project and its community.

## Project-specific priority

Because this repository models healthcare operations, **privacy, data provenance, clinical boundaries, auditability, and human oversight are mandatory governance principles**, not optional conventions.
